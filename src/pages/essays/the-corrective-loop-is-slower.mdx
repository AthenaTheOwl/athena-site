LOOP RECORD — SEPTEMBER 2026

Forcing side, measured in months:
Strait of Hormuz, one Monday in August: six vessels reported transiting, against roughly 130 to 140 a day before the war.
Federal Reserve, 16 September: its first rate increase in more than three years, aimed at "combating inflation brought on by spiraling oil prices and other factors."
Conventional DRAM contract prices, one quarter: up about 93 to 98 percent.
Texas grid queue: about 474 GW of requests, more than five times the state's record peak.

Corrective side, measured in years:
Gas turbines: reservations now taken for 2031 delivery; 3 GW shipped in a quarter against 20 GW signed.
Turbine factory capacity: about 20 GW a year, planned to reach 30 GW by 2030.
HBM contract pricing: next reset, 2027.

Time for the corrective loop to close: no field.

An oil shock raises inflation. Inflation raises rates. Higher rates raise the cost of capital for exactly the multi-year, capital-intensive projects, fabs, reactors, transmission, turbines, that would relieve the bottleneck.

The shortage raises the return on new supply and the hurdle rate for building it in the same motion.

That is the control-theory signature of a constraint that persists, and the empty field is its length.

## FINDING ONE: THE FORCING LOOP RUNS IN MONTHS.

Every entry on the forcing side of the record moved within a quarter.

A strait went from roughly 130 transits a day to six. A central bank that had not raised rates in three years raised them, and signalled another. Memory contract prices nearly doubled between one quarter and the next. A state's grid queue filled with requests five times its record peak, and the governor ordered an audit.

None of these is slow. Oil moves on a headline. Rates move on a vote. Contract prices move on the next negotiation. Queue requests move on a form that costs nothing to file.

The disturbance arrives at the speed of paperwork.

## FINDING TWO: THE CORRECTIVE LOOP RUNS IN YEARS.

The response is real, and it is measured in the only unit the physical world accepts.

The largest gas-turbine maker is taking reservations for 2031. It shipped three gigawatts of turbines in a quarter and signed twenty gigawatts of orders and reservations, and its plan to expand factory capacity from about twenty gigawatts a year to thirty by 2030 drew an analyst's verdict that "the jury's out."

Memory repricing waits for the annual HBM contracts, and the 2027 round is the next one. Reactors take years. Transmission takes years and then a hearing.

And the rate environment the shortage produced is the environment in which each of those has to be financed.

·   ·

A thermostat wired to a furnace that takes four years to light will not hold the room at temperature.

It will oscillate.

The people in the room will buy sweaters, which is what compression, paging, behind-the-meter generation and flexible load are.

## FINDING THREE: THE SHORTAGE RAISES THE HURDLE RATE FOR ITS OWN CURE.

The Federal Reserve's September statement of purpose, as CNBC reported it, was to combat "inflation brought on by spiraling oil prices." Sixteen of eighteen participants expected another increase before year-end.

Follow the causal chain. An energy shock raises headline inflation. Central banks in both reserve-currency blocs raise rates. The discount rate rises for every long-duration project, and the longest-duration projects in the economy are the ones that would relieve the shortage: a turbine delivered in 2031, a fab, a reactor, a transmission line.

The price signal says build.

The rate signal says wait.

Both are correct, and the second one binds on the projects the first one is asking for.

## FINDING FOUR: EFFICIENCY ARRIVES AND GETS EATEN.

The counterargument is that the technology is getting cheaper fast enough to outrun the constraint, and the numbers say it is getting cheaper fast.

Epoch AI measured how quickly the price of reaching a given capability has fallen, and found declines ranging "from 9x to 900x per year" depending on the milestone. The price of GPT-4's performance on PhD-level science questions fell by 40 times a year. Epoch's own caveat: the fastest drops were recent, "so it's less clear that those will persist."

Over the same period, Epoch notes, inference revenue at the major labs has been growing at "3x per year or more."

Efficiency gains are being consumed by demand faster than they arrive. Cheaper inference does not reduce the power the industry draws. It expands what people ask of it until the bill returns to the ceiling.

A fortyfold annual price decline is a spectacular rate of improvement.

It is losing.

## FINDING FIVE: EVERY TRADE IN THIS CLUSTER PAYS BECAUSE THE LOOP IS SLOW.

The essays beside this one describe four assets. Curtailable load. An island of private generation. A position in a queue. A chip or a model designed to need less memory.

Each is a bet that pays specifically while the corrective loop is open. Curtailability is worth most while firm capacity is scarce. The island is worth building while the queue is years long. The queue position is valuable while the queue is. The memory-free design wins while memory is short.

They are all the same trade, written in four dialects.

And each carries the same risk: the day the loop closes, the premium goes to zero, and the loop tends to close all at once.

·   ·

A control engineer would recognise every chart in this essay: a fast disturbance, a slow actuator, and an operator who keeps turning the dial because nothing seems to happen.

Nothing seems to happen for four years. Then all of it arrives together, and the oscillation begins.

## THE EXPLANATIONS AFTERWARD

The findings have their exits. Each gets its due.

The first explanation was that high prices cure high prices, and this is what a functioning market looks like mid-cycle.

Usually true, and the mechanism is the finding, not a rebuttal to it. High prices cure high prices when the supply response is fast relative to the demand impulse. Here the supply response is a turbine factory and the demand impulse is a procurement decision. The cure works. What matters is whether it works before the patient has made five years of decisions.

The second explanation was that the oil shock is geopolitical and will normalise, so the rate environment relaxes.

Institutional forecasts split wide on exactly that, and this essay cannot settle it. What it can say is that the people closest to the physical flows started treating the disruption as a baseline, not a premium. When the people with the tankers change their planning assumption, that is data about planning, whether or not it is data about prices.

**Objection:** So the answer is to build anyway.

**Reply:** The answer is to notice which of your bets require the loop to close on schedule, and price the ones that do not.

**Objection:** That sounds like doing nothing.

**Reply:** It is the opposite. Every trade in Finding Five is a decision that pays specifically because the loop is slow.

The third explanation was that Jevons cuts the other way, because cheaper inference expands the market and the market funds the buildout.

It does, and that is the loop again. The funding arrives as capex with a multi-year lead time, and the demand arrives as an API call this afternoon. Both are real. Only one of them has a construction schedule.

The fourth explanation was the inversion, and control theory supplies it as readily as it supplies the thesis: the corrective loop is not slow, it is lagged, and a lagged loop overshoots. Turbine orders are running at seven times shipments. Capital is arriving in half-trillion-dollar platforms. Memory is about to reprice. On that reading, the danger is not a shortage that persists but a glut that arrives in 2028 or 2029, and every trade that pays while the loop is open gets caught when it slams shut.

The case is strong, and it is the other half of the same diagram.

**Objection:** Then the glut is coming.

**Reply:** Late.

The evidence that settles which half dominates is visible in order books before it is visible in prices: turbine order cancellations, falling conventional memory contract prices after the 2027 repricing, capacity auctions clearing below the cap. If those appear, the overshoot has started and the inversion wins. The failure mode belongs in the same sentence: persistence and overshoot are not rivals but two phases of one lagged system, and the expensive mistake is to price only one of them. A bet sized for a permanent shortage is caught by the glut. A bet sized for the glut misses the years before it.

## WHAT THE PORTFOLIO DOES ABOUT IT

[grid-silicon](https://github.com/AthenaTheOwl/grid-silicon) measures the gap between announced and energised megawatts, which is the corrective loop's lag expressed as a number per site.

[power-ppa-forge](https://github.com/AthenaTheOwl/power-ppa-forge) allocates a plant among buyers who will not disclose what they need, which is the contracting problem that exists because the plant is scarce and slow.

[wafer-to-watt](https://github.com/AthenaTheOwl/wafer-to-watt) traces the chain the other direction: one buyer's 50,000 systems are signed for, while 120,000 custom-accelerator wafers are inferred from a channel check and nobody signed for them on the record. The distance between a signature and an inference is where the forcing loop's numbers come from, and the repo's job is to keep the two apart.

None of the three closes the loop. They measure how far open it is, which is the only honest thing a small repository can do about a constraint that takes years to relieve.

Watch the clocks, not the prices.
