SLOT RESERVATION — GAS TURBINES, ONE MANUFACTURER, SECOND QUARTER 2026

Backlog of orders and slot reservations: 116 GW, up from 100 GW a quarter earlier.
Expected by year-end: 125 GW.
Delivery year now being reserved: 2031.
Turbines shipped in the quarter: 3 GW.
Orders and slot reservations signed in the quarter: 20 GW.
Annual manufacturing capacity: about 20 GW, planned to reach 30 GW by 2030.
Transferable to another buyer: no field.

At three gigawatts shipped against twenty reserved, the line grew almost seven times faster than it moved. The manufacturer's chief executive said the company was taking reservations for 2031 deliveries.

The reservation is the asset. Whether it can be sold is the field nobody filled in, and it is the whole of this essay.

## FINDING ONE: THE VALUE ACCRUES TO WHOEVER ENTERED EARLY.

When a scarce input is allocated by queue and administrative review instead of by price, the queue position itself becomes the valuable asset, decoupled from the underlying commodity, and value accrues to whoever entered early, not to whoever pays most.

That is a different economy from the one most people model. Price rations by willingness to pay; a queue rations by patience and paperwork, and patience turns out to be transferable in ways the regulator did not intend.

The lineage is old: spectrum licences, airport landing slots, taxi medallions, administratively scarce rights to access a network, decoupled from the underlying asset. When connection is scarcer than capacity, the right to connect prices independently of the thing being connected, and often above it.

The list of places it applies is long and consistent: water rights in stressed basins, port and berth slots, spectrum, environmental permits, priority review vouchers, airport gates, transformer manufacturing slots, clinical trial site capacity, foundry tape-out slots. Anywhere a chokepoint is rationed first-come.

The permit is the scarce good. The plant is an expense you incur later to justify it.

## FINDING TWO: A RESERVATION IS AN OPTION, AND THE BACKLOG IS PARTLY OPTIONS.

The turbine record at the top counts orders and slot reservations together. The distinction matters. An order is a commitment to buy a machine. A slot reservation is the right to buy one later, paid for now because lead time has outrun the buyer's planning horizon.

When lead time exceeds the planning horizon, the market starts selling the right to buy, and a backlog stops being a pure order book. Part of it is a book of options.

The same structure fits anything oversubscribed: GPU inference capacity reserved a year out, contract manufacturing slots for drugs that have not finished trials, interconnection positions.

An analyst quoted in the same report said the market expected a year-end backlog of 130 to 140 gigawatts, and that on the company's plan to reach 30 gigawatts of annual capacity by 2030, "the jury's out."

A backlog is an order book.

It is not a delivery schedule.

·   ·

The purest queue asset is a numbered ticket at a deli counter.

It costs nothing. It cannot be sold. It expires when you leave the shop.

Every argument in this essay is about what happens when the ticket stops being free, starts being sellable, or outlives the shop.

## FINDING THREE: BUY THE CLEARANCE, NOT THE PLANT.

The cleanest version of the trade is not queuing at all. When permitting and queue time dominate build time, the value sits in already-cleared assets: acquire the regulatory clearance, not the plant. Restarted reactors and offtake from existing nuclear capacity are the visible cases. The general form is a fund that buys permitted but underused industrial assets.

The inversion that creates the arbitrage fits in one line: across sectors, permitting timelines have grown faster than construction timelines.

Crypto miners were repriced for their grid interconnections instead of their hardware, which is the market saying the same thing with money. Where a regulator rations access through a multi-year queue, the option on the slot appreciates independently of the business that justified joining.

Buy the permit. Build the thing later.

## FINDING FOUR: A FREE QUEUE STOPS ENCODING DEMAND.

In August, Texas's grid queue held about 474 gigawatts of requests, which the governor described as "more than five times Texas' record peak electricity demand," with "approximately 90 percent of the new power requests" coming from data centres. He called for an audit of every data centre in the queue.

When entry to a queue is free and the resource is five times oversubscribed, the queue stops encoding demand and starts encoding optionality. Everyone joins because joining costs nothing, so the number at the end of the line was never a forecast: it is a count of free options. An audit is the state asking which of the options are real.

The fix is proof of seriousness, not price: bring your own power, your own water, your own capital. The federal version is under consideration at FERC, which is asking whether large loads that "agree to be flexible and curtail usage should be able to move through interconnection studies faster," and whether those studies "could be completed within 60 days."

The failure mode arrives in the same breath. Proof-of-seriousness requirements calcify into incumbency moats. Only balance sheets that can pre-fund power get to build, which is a capital test dressed as an engineering one.

Most projects in these queues eventually withdraw, which means much of the observed scarcity is phantom.

You can own a perfect place in a line to nowhere.

## FINDING FIVE: THE ASSET HAS POLITICAL DURATION, NOT LEGAL DURATION.

Every form of this asset carries the same risk, and it arrives in several dialects.

Regulators designed interconnection queues to be non-speculative, and can void transferability overnight.

Many queue positions are legally non-transferable, or void on change of use, so the option evaporates exactly when you try to exercise it.

Permits often lapse on change of use, and regulators tighten conditions retroactively when they notice an asset repriced ten times over. The arbitrage invites the rule change that closes it.

Reform aimed at clearing the backlog would also destroy the scarcity the trade depends on. A rule change is a 100 percent drawdown, not a markdown.

FERC is already in that business. Its December 2025 order on co-located load in PJM rewrote who may use an existing grid connection and on what terms.

This is a bet on continued dysfunction, and bets on dysfunction have a poor long-run record and excellent medium-run returns.

## FINDING SIX: THE CONSTRAINT RELOCATES.

Clear one line and the next one becomes the binding one.

A buyer who leaves the grid queue to build on-site generation joins the turbine queue, where the delivery year being reserved is 2031. A buyer who wins an interconnection finds the transformer line behind it. Removing a delivery constraint expands the addressable population far more than improving the payload does, and then the bottleneck moves to manufacturing volume and budgets.

The asset was never one slot.

It was being early to whichever line is currently the short one.

Every queue is a promise that the resource will exist when your number is called.

The ticket is only as good as the building being finished, and nobody in the line can see the building site.

## THE EXPLANATIONS AFTERWARD

The findings have their exits. Each gets its due.

The first explanation was that a secondary market will form anyway, because it always does.

Probably so. Secondary markets emerge whether or not the regulator intends them: financing structures that underwrite queue position as collateral, brokerage for co-location rights inside new tariffs. Nobody doubts that the market forms. What matters is whether it survives the first year in which it is visible enough to embarrass someone.

The second explanation was that a capacity-options market is the mature version of this, and maturity is stabilising.

The turbine market is already there, and the failure mode is unchanged: regulators may treat position trading as speculation and ban it, as FERC has flirted with for interconnection.

**Objection:** Then the asset is uninvestable.

**Reply:** The asset is investable with a political duration attached, which is a different discipline from a legal one. Price the memo.

**Objection:** Nobody prices the memo.

**Reply:** That is the trade.

The third explanation was that routing around the queue entirely beats standing in it.

That is the neighbouring essay, and it carries its own bill. Vertical integration beats waiting when the queue is real, and the acquired asset's value is the permission it carries, not its physical substance. But bypass works only until the regulator prices it: retroactive standby charges, exit fees, or curtailment obligations can strand the asset overnight.

The fourth explanation was the inversion, and a regulator would make it gladly: queue positions are not an asset class, they are a bug in the middle of being fixed. Texas is auditing its queue, FERC is writing readiness and flexibility criteria, and PJM's co-location rules are being rewritten by order. On that reading, anyone buying positions now is buying the top of an arbitrage that has already been scheduled for closure.

The case is strong, and every regulatory move this summer supports it.

**Objection:** Then sell the position now.

**Reply:** Probably.

The evidence that settles it is how the reforms re-sequence the lines. If queues are re-ordered by readiness and flexibility, and positions become non-transferable, the asset goes to zero and the inversion wins. If the reforms stall, or re-sequencing leaves early positions intact, the medium run continues. The failure mode sits beside the claim and softens it: the reform that closes one queue opens another behind it, as the turbine book shows, so the lesson of Finding Six survives even if this asset dies. Being early to the short line is a skill. Owning one ticket is a position.

## THE COST OF STANDING IN THE WRONG LINE

[robust-siting-lab](https://github.com/AthenaTheOwl/robust-siting-lab) is this essay with numbers attached. A data centre gets announced on a campus; the lab scores three candidate sites and finds one carries 2.2 percent more expected cost than the cheapest option. Siting under uncertainty is exactly the decision a queue position forces, because the site you choose determines the line you join, and the line determines whether the megawatts arrive in year three or year eight.

[grid-silicon](https://github.com/AthenaTheOwl/grid-silicon) holds the other half: 1,200 megawatts announced, 120 energised, 1,080 on a form. That gap is the asset this essay is about, measured from the outside.

Own the permit, not the plant.

Check which line you are in before you congratulate yourself on how early you arrived.
