A decision record answers why this path was chosen while the answer is still fresh. Each product repo keeps `decisions/DEC-*.md` files. The shared contract is `ops/schemas/decision.schema.json`; repo validators check the YAML frontmatter.

A decision is a reason with a return address.

## What the record holds

The schema requires `id`, `spec`, `requirement`, `date`, `status`, `reversible`, `decision`, `alternatives`, `rationale`, `evidence` and `rollback`. Each alternative names a label and why it lost. Evidence references say what the author consulted; the rollback field says how to undo the choice.

`reversible` is a boolean, not an estimate of the cost. The rollback text has to carry the practical limit. A reversible flag beside a migration nobody can execute is an optimistic form.

## A concrete example

Supplier-risk-rag-agent's `DEC-CIT-002-citations-carry-filing-level-identifiers.md` chooses a seven-field citation record. It keeps the filing's CIK, accession and section beside the span and chunk references, so the citation can identify its source after the in-memory corpus changes.

Its alternatives include storing only span text and chunk id, loading filing identifiers only at render time, and making the record mutable. The decision rejects each for a stated audit reason. It marks the change hard to reverse because the shape has several consumers.

The sibling `DEC-CIT-002-amendment-reversibility-mitigation.md` names a forward path: introduce `CitationV2` beside the existing shape, migrate consumers across a release cycle, then deprecate V1. It does not claim that migration has happened. The amendment preserves the earlier decision and adds the route the first record lacked.

## Why a separate record

Commit messages can carry reasons too. A separate decision gives the reason a stable name, fields for alternatives and rollback, and links other records can use. It does not automatically survive a history rewrite: a decision file in the same Git history needs the same custody and backup as other records.

The distinction is purpose, not immortality.

## What to audit

A decision audit should check unresolved requirements, broken spec references, proposals left open and choices whose evidence no longer applies. Coverage and validity are separate findings. A well-formed decision can be wrong; an old decision can still be right.

The portfolio's coverage reports expose missing links. The next review has to read the surviving rationale and ask what observation would reopen it. A filled field is the start of that review, not its verdict.
